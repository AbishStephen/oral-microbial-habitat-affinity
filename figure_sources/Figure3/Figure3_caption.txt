Figure 3 | Recurrent domain annotations encompass heterogeneous protein sequence clusters and domain combinations. (a) For 13 Pfam families studied in the candidate/locus analysis, a domain can occur across a larger number of oral bacterial genera (teal circles) than any single sequence cluster (coral diamonds). Grey bars show the number of sequence clusters within that Pfam family. (b) Selected high-breadth MMseqs2 clusters bearing HxlR, LCM or TIR_2 annotations, arranged by the taxa in which the corresponding protein sequences occurred. Dot colours correspond to bacterial phyla and line segments connect the observed genus membership of each cluster; intervening taxa are not implied to contain the cluster. The plots display only the highest-ranked clusters for readability, not all clusters. Clusters derive from MMseqs2 easy-linclust at 50% sequence identity and 80% coverage and therefore do not necessarily represent distinct evolutionary orthologues or biochemical specificities. (c) Dominant combinations of Pfam annotations among proteins carrying five representative domains. Percentages are calculated over the protein counts in the source annotation data. Architectures are represented as co-annotations and do not establish N-to-C domain order or transcriptional units. (d) Genus-balanced omnibus tests of directional functional-repertoire coherence across six annotation layers: Pfam (4,850 features), COG (1,575), EC (246), KEGG modules (251), KEGG pathways (126) and 50% identity protein clusters (5,745). All displayed permutation P values are nonsignificant. These results do not exclude sparse repeated signals within particular feature families, and the current 14-feature shortlist did not pass the genome-wide maxT multiple-testing correction. Genomic observations were aggregated within HMT, with no individual reference assembly treated as an independent ecological replicate.
