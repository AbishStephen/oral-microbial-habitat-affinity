Figure 4 | Conservation of gene orientation across heterogeneous protein families. (A) True gene coordinates, strand orientation and CDS lengths from Prokka GFF files for six representative genomic neighbourhoods containing linked HxlR (PF01638) and SnoaL_2 (PF12680) domains. Tracks are aligned by the midpoint of the linked pair; the Streptococcus HMT-411 track is reverse-complemented for visualization. Coloured arrows encode annotated domain-bearing genes, while grey and purple arrows encode surrounding CDSs and putatively mobile genetic elements. The single Neisseria locus has a 2,124-bp gap and is same-strand, unlike the tighter divergent motifs. (B) Intergenic separation of all 67 linked HxlR–SnoaL_2 genome records from five genera, 66 of which were divergent within 500 bp; the remaining Neisseria example was loosely linked. (C) Actual representative genomic contexts of two distinct locus architectures restricted to Fusobacterium (HxlR–WHD_ArnR1, 38 genomes in the complete scan) and Actinomyces (SnoaL_2–Polyketide_cyc2, 35 genomes). (D) The 67 linked HxlR–SnoaL_2 genomes contain eight MMseqs2 sequence-cluster pair types; just one pair type occurred in more than one genus. This is consistent with recurrent organization despite protein sequence diversity, not necessarily independent evolution, shared regulation, or convergence. Genus-balanced linkage enrichment with plaque specialization was not statistically significant (two-sided P≈0.217). All observed CDS spans and linkage distances are from the accompanying GFF-derived data, while product annotation and functional inference remain putative.
