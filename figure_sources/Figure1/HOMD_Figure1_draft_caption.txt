Fig. 1 | Oral habitat specialization is represented across 13 bacterial genera spanning six phyla. (a) Branch-length phylogram inferred from 18 conserved marker proteins for 47 Human Microbial Taxa (HMTs), ordered as in the input tree and coloured by their plaque-associated (coral) or mucosa-associated (teal) ecological classification. The horizontal dot-bar shows the HMT-specific median log2 plaque-to-mucosal site-affinity score derived from publicly available HOMD abundance summaries (SUBP and SUPP versus BMU and TDO; a small pseudocount was applied). Solid affinity symbols designate high-confidence classifications (n=32); open symbols designate moderate-confidence classifications (n=15). Numbers at right indicate the reference genomes supporting each HMT (1,121 in total), not independently sampled ecological observations. (b) Numbers of HMTs assigned to the plaque and mucosal classes in each of the 13 genera; each genus includes representatives of both classes. Phylum colours are used only as taxonomic guides. The tree is display-rooted and no ancestral habitat states or independent evolutionary transitions are inferred. Habitat categories are intentionally enriched for strong specialists rather than being an unbiased census of oral bacteria.
